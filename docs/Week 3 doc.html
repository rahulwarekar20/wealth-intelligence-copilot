Agent primer

The agent in one sentence, its design card, and how it meets the three rules

1. The one-liner

My agent helps a self-directed investor decide whether to buy, hold, trim or sell a position in a web app (desktop and phone), replacing the hours of reading 10-K filings, checking quotes and news, and updating a spreadsheet of position sizes it takes to make one informed decision today. It resolves the company, reads the relevant 10-K sections and SEC financials, checks today's price action and news, weighs the position against the investor's own holdings, risk profile and concentration limit, and returns a cited recommendation on its own using 5 tools. It hands off to the investor before anything is written to their portfolio, and whenever its specialists disagree or evidence is missing. It never places a trade. I'll know it works when an investor can go from question to a decision they would act on in under 2 minutes, with every cited point traceable to its source, 8 times out of 10.

The five tools: SEC EDGAR (XBRL financials and 10-K text), Finnhub (quotes, news, fundamentals, earnings calendar), portfolio valuation, private-document search, and the investor profile with other assets.

2. Agent design card

FieldFill in
Agent goal Takes an investor's question about a stock or their portfolio and returns a cited buy / hold / trim / sell recommendation that fits their own holdings and risk profile.
Where do people use it? A web app (desktop and phone browser): chat with the copilot, plus dashboard pages for portfolio, strategy, alerts and the decision journal.
What steps does it take, in order?
  1. Supervisor reads the question and recent turns, resolves the company and picks which specialists are needed.
  2. Specialists run in parallel: portfolio (position size, concentration), evidence (10-K sections, SEC financials, news, private documents), market data (price, momentum).
  3. Guardrails check each specialist's citations and measure direction, agreement and coverage.
  4. Composer writes the answer with key points, sources and a confidence that code caps when evidence conflicts or is missing.
  5. The answer is journaled and shown; the investor decides what to do.
What can it actually do? Look-up SEC EDGAR filings and XBRL financials · Look-up Finnhub quotes, news and earnings · Look-up portfolio valuation and concentration · Look-up search of the investor's uploaded documents · Look-up investor profile and other assets · Changes appends its decisions and alerts to the journal (its own audit log only).
What does it need to remember? Within a session, the last 12 chat turns (so "should I trim it?" knows what "it" is). Across sessions: holdings, risk profile, other assets, every past decision with its price at the time, and the latest strategy (reused for 30 min).
What should it never do? Never place trades or move money, never change holdings or the profile without the investor confirming, never show one user's portfolio to another, and never present a figure or source it didn't actually retrieve as verified.
Human-in-the-loop Before any write: holdings, imports (row-by-row preview, then confirm) and profile changes need the investor's approval. Final approval: every recommendation is advice the investor acts on themselves. Answers with disagreeing or missing evidence show capped confidence and the reason. The investor can intervene with a follow-up question, Try again, Refresh strategy, or by editing holdings.
What happens when something breaks? Short LLM rate limits are retried automatically, and a malformed reply is retried once. If a data source or specialist fails, the answer still arrives but names what's missing and caps confidence at 0.5. If the whole answer fails, the chat shows the error with a Try again button, and an expired session sends the user to sign in again with a message saying why.
How do you know it worked? The investor goes from question to a decision they would act on in under 2 minutes, with every cited point verified, 8 times out of 10.

3. Rule 1: task completion, not single-shot accuracy

Success is the investor reaching a decision, often over several turns ("What are Microsoft's biggest risks?" → "Should I trim it?"), not one good model answer. It is measured end to end:

  • Time to decision: from the first question to the final answer.
  • Citation integrity: share of answers with every citation verified. The backend already records verified, cited and withheld counts on each answer.
  • Outcome: whether the call holds up afterwards. The track record compares the price at the decision with today's.

5. Rule 3: write actions deserve a human

AutonomousReads

Filings, quotes, news, portfolio valuation, document search, alert checks.

Human-approvedWrites

  • Adding or removing holdings.
  • Broker imports: previewed row by row, with a second confirmation before holdings are replaced.
  • Saving the profile and other assets.

NeverTrades

Every Buy or Sell is advice the investor carries out elsewhere.

The one autonomous write: the agent appends its own decisions and alerts to the journal. That's an internal audit log, not a change to anything the user owns.
