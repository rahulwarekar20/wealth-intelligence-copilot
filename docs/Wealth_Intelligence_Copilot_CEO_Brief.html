Wealth Intelligence Copilot

CEO Brief and Demo Guide

Product value, user workflow, architecture and AI implementation
11 October 2026

Wealth Intelligence Copilot helps an investor understand a stock decision in the context of their own holdings. It brings portfolio exposure, company filings, market signals and private research into one conversational workflow, then presents an answer with supporting and opposing evidence.

Executive conclusion. The application is a working decision support prototype with a credible demonstration of contextual research. Its next milestone should be a measured pilot that validates answer quality, traceability and user time savings before investment in production scale. It does not execute trades, and the current implementation does not establish superior investment returns.

1 Business purpose and benefits

Investors often move between a portfolio tracker, a quote service, lengthy SEC filings, news articles and their own notes before forming a view. Those tools provide information, but the user must connect the company’s prospects to their personal exposure. WIC combines that work into a single question such as “Should I buy more Apple?”

The useful distinction is context. A company can have strong fundamentals while already representing too much of the user’s portfolio. WIC can bring both facts into the same answer. The user can inspect the rationale and filing links rather than relying only on a recommendation label.

AudiencePractical usePotential benefit to validate
Self-directed investorResearch a stock, compare alternatives and assess concentration.Less time assembling information; clearer tradeoffs.
Advisor or analystPrepare a first-pass review using holdings and internal research.Faster preparation and more consistent evidence presentation.
Wealth platform teamOffer an explanatory research layer alongside existing portfolio tools.More useful engagement and a reusable decision support workflow.

These are candidate users and business benefits, not measured customer outcomes. Possible commercial models include a research subscription or an advisor workspace; neither billing nor an enterprise advisor workflow is implemented.

“We help users understand what a company’s evidence means for their own portfolio, while keeping the sources visible and the final decision with the user.”

3 How to use each tab

Open http://localhost:3000 for the local demo and sign in or create an account. Seed the demo data beforehand to show five holdings: AAPL, GOOGL, SPY, JPM and MSFT. The current sidebar has four tabs.

Dashboard

  1. Review total priced portfolio value and the holdings list.
  2. Add a holding using its ticker, quantity, cost basis and account type. Cost basis represents a per-share cost in the valuation code.
  3. Inspect the current price, position value, portfolio weight and gain or loss versus cost.
  4. Look for large position weights and concentration warnings. Remove a holding when it no longer belongs in the demonstration portfolio.

Interpretation. Market value equals quantity multiplied by the quote. Weight equals position value divided by total priced portfolio value. A missing price excludes that holding from value and weight calculations and is reported to the user. Quotes are cached, so “live” means provider-fed rather than a streaming real-time guarantee.

The dashboard correlation figures use approximately 30 daily closing prices. They currently correlate price levels rather than returns and align series by length rather than explicit trading dates. Present these as exploratory indicators, not an institutional risk model.

Analyse Portfolio

  1. Click Analyse My Portfolio.
  2. Review Portfolio Health, diversification, top risk and top opportunity.
  3. Read Priority Actions, then the Position-by-Position Strategy.
  4. Open available SEC source links and use Refresh to request a new analysis.

The agent combines priced holdings with market evidence and SEC financial highlights. It aggregates the same ticker across accounts. It does not run the full private-document and 10-K passage retrieval used by individual stock chat analysis. The UI indicates a 30 to 60 second wait; actual latency depends on portfolio size, network and provider availability.

Ask Copilot

  1. Ask a direct question, such as “What are Microsoft’s biggest risks?” or “Should I buy more Apple?”
  2. Review the direct answer, key points, action, confidence, positives, risks, numerical metrics and sources.
  3. Ask a follow-up such as “Should I sell it?” to demonstrate recent conversation context.
  4. Compare stocks with “AAPL or GOOGL — which looks stronger?”
  5. Ask “How healthy is my whole portfolio?” to route to portfolio strategy, or ask a retirement question to use the retirement agent.

For retirement, provide a time horizon, age, desired annual expenses and current savings contribution. The agent checks recent history before asking again. Treat the result as an assumption-based estimate. The model uses rules of thumb and stated return or inflation assumptions rather than a verified financial planning calculator.

Confidence is not a probability of profit. It is an output score influenced by model judgment and a simple code guardrail. Citation labels help inspection but do not guarantee that every generated claim is faithfully supported.

Documents

  1. Select a text-based PDF, TXT or CSV of up to 5 MB.
  2. Click Upload Document and check the successful text extraction message.
  3. Return to Ask Copilot and ask about a company explicitly named in the document.
  4. Look for the document filename in cited evidence and the sources list. Use Delete to remove it from future retrieval.

Public SEC filings are fetched automatically; use uploads for private research. Scanned PDFs are unsupported because there is no OCR. CSV files are treated as text, not imported as structured portfolios. Uploading a statement does not automatically add its holdings to the dashboard.

Deleting a document removes the stored text used for future searches; earlier saved decision records may still contain excerpts or generated statements. Retirement and Journal routes redirect to Ask Copilot and are not separate tabs.

5 Market positioning

Portfolio tracking, automated investing and AI-generated strategies already exist. WIC’s defensible positioning is the combination of portfolio-aware questions, SEC evidence, private company research and explicit supporting and opposing signals. This is a product design distinction, not proof of exclusive features or a durable competitive moat.

Representative productPublicly described focusWIC positioning
Empower Personal DashboardFinancial overview, budgeting, investment checkup and retirement scenario planning. [M1]Emphasizes conversational company research tied to manually recorded holdings.
WealthfrontAutomated investing and personalized planning through its Path advice engine. [M2]Provides research and explanations; has no custody, tax-loss harvesting or trade execution integration.
ComposerAI-assisted discovery, construction and operation of automated trading strategies. [M3]Explains stock and portfolio decisions using retrieved evidence; does not deploy trading strategies.

These comparisons concern the products’ published emphasis. They do not assert that a competitor lacks every WIC feature. Competitor material was checked on 11 October 2026; features can change.

What could become an advantage. A validated corpus of financial questions, strong citation evaluation, better portfolio context and permission-aware private research could make the experience difficult to replicate well. Model access and agent orchestration alone are widely available. WIC currently lacks the account connectivity and mature planning infrastructure offered by established platforms.

6 Architecture and technology

The application uses a three-tier web architecture: a Next.js browser interface, a FastAPI application server and PostgreSQL persistence. The backend is a modular monolith with API, services, repositories, agents and models organized into separate Python modules. Specialist agents are logical components inside that server, not separately deployed microservices.

A chat request carries a question and recent history to the authenticated API. The server resolves intent, selects a route, gathers holdings and external evidence, invokes the relevant model stages, normalizes the response and persists successful stock or strategy results. The frontend renders specialized response cards.

7 How the application is agentic

WIC is a bounded agent workflow. A model-based supervisor interprets the request, identifies up to three tickers from names and recent context, and selects specialist agents. Application code verifies candidate tickers against holdings or a quote lookup before dispatch. Unclear requests can produce a clarification instead of an invented analysis.

ComponentMethodResponsibility
SupervisorSmall LLMIntent, company resolution and agent selection.
Portfolio AgentDeterministic codePosition weight and concentration; high above 25%, medium above 15%.
Market Data AgentDeterministic codeDaily quote movement, momentum and intraday range.
Evidence AgentRetrieval and small LLMSEC, market, news and document context; supporting and opposing signals.
Recommendation ComposerLarge LLM and codeAnswer, action, explanation, confidence and output normalization.
Portfolio Strategy AgentLarge LLMAll-position review, health assessment and priorities.
Retirement AgentLarge LLMContextual savings and retirement estimates.

The single-stock LangGraph is specialists → composer → END. Specialist work runs concurrently using a thread pool; database workers receive separate SQLAlchemy sessions. Stock comparisons run per-ticker pipelines concurrently and add a small-model comparison verdict. Portfolio strategy and retirement are routed outside this two-node graph.

LangChain’s actual role. LangGraph supplies StateGraph and the execution structure. langchain-core is listed as a dependency. The inspected implementation uses custom HTTPX model calls and custom retrieval functions; it does not use a LangChain vector store, document loader chain, agent tool loop or standard retriever pipeline.

The app is agentic because it selects and coordinates specialized work based on intent and combines the results. Its autonomy is limited to analysis. There is no open-ended planning loop, background portfolio monitoring, self-directed trading, durable graph checkpointing or long-term agent memory implemented in this workflow. Recent chat history is passed into prompts rather than stored as a dedicated memory system.

8 RAG and retrieval strategy

Retrieval augmented generation means supplying retrieved information to a model before it generates an answer. WIC implements this pattern with official financial data and keyword-selected text passages. It does not train the model on the user’s documents or perform embedding-based nearest-neighbor search.

Public filing retrieval

  1. Resolve ticker to SEC CIK using the SEC company ticker dataset.
  2. Fetch company facts and filing submissions. Build annual financial context from XBRL facts, including up to three years of annual figures.
  3. Fetch the latest 10-K HTML and extract Item 1A Risk Factors and Item 7 MD&A.
  4. Select question-relevant passages and attach company, filing year, section labels and source URLs.
  5. Insert financials and selected passages into the Evidence Agent prompt. Pass its analysis and raw metrics to the composer.

The service also discovers filing metadata and links including 10-Q filings. The narrative retrieval path described here specifically selects 10-K sections; it is not a comprehensive 10-Q narrative analysis pipeline.

Current chunking strategy

SourceText unit and limitsOverlap and metadata
SEC filing sectionsNewline-separated paragraphs of at least 120 characters. Selected passages are capped at 600 characters, trimming at a word boundary. The evidence caller requests two passages per section.No explicit overlap. Citation includes ticker, 10-K fiscal year and item label.
Uploaded documentsWhole extracted text stored, capped at 500,000 characters. Retrieval creates a window of about 150 characters before and after the matched term, plus the term itself.No persistent chunk index or overlap setting. Filename is the source label.

This is on-demand passage selection rather than a precomputed token chunking pipeline. No embedding model, vector dimension, similarity threshold or vector index is used.

Current retrieval strategy

SEC text. The query is lowercased and tokenized into alphabetic words of at least three characters. Common words are removed. Each paragraph is scored by the sum of occurrences of the remaining query terms. Higher scores rank first; original order breaks ties. If nothing matches, the service uses the opening paragraphs. The caller retrieves up to four passages across the two sections.

Private documents. The service searches only the authenticated user’s documents, newest first. Case-insensitive whole-word matching uses the ticker, company name and a company name with its corporate suffix removed. It returns at most four excerpts and only one excerpt per document. These excerpts are selected by company mentions, not ranked by the question’s meaning.

Freshness. Quotes use a five-minute in-memory cache with last-known-price fallback on failure. General market evidence is reused for ten minutes. SEC financials are cached for 24 hours. Parsed filing sections are held in memory by accession, with a maximum of 50 cached entries.

Why this design is useful for a prototype. It is inexpensive, inspectable and avoids an embedding service. Its limitations are missed synonyms, generic fallback passages, clipped context and private-document selection biased toward recent documents and first mentions. PDF extraction does not retain page-level provenance in the stored retrieval output.

Proposed next design. Preserve filing sections, document IDs and page or location metadata; create token-aware chunks with evaluated overlap; combine lexical search with embeddings; rerank candidates; enforce user scope before retrieval; and evaluate citation support and retrieval recall on representative questions. Chunk sizes, overlap, embedding provider and top-k should be selected through experiments, not presented as existing settings.

9 Models and API integrations

The active provider is Groq, using its OpenAI-compatible chat completions endpoint. The model identifiers begin with openai/, but requests go to Groq, not the OpenAI API. The bedrock_client.py file is a deprecated compatibility wrapper that also calls Groq; it does not invoke AWS Bedrock or Claude.

Temperature is 0.1. Calls request structured JSON through prompts, then parse and normalize it in application code. This is not strict provider-enforced JSON schema validation. Token budgets vary by stage; malformed or empty JSON responses can be retried once with a doubled budget. Short rate-limit delays of up to four seconds are retried; longer limits return an error. 10 Trust and operational limitations

Implemented controls include JWT-protected routes, password hashing, user-filtered holdings and document queries, validated inputs, upload size and type checks, restricted CORS and response security headers. Startup checks warn about missing keys and placeholder secrets. These controls are useful foundations, not a certification of production security.

The composer attaches source links and numerical metrics from retrieved data, and it enforces a confidence cap of 0.4 when the evidence score is below 0.4. Failed final synthesis is reported rather than logged as a successful placeholder recommendation. A failed Evidence Agent can still pass raw financial data to the composer; this is a degraded analysis, not a complete evidence review.

How to explain the evidence score honestly

The code calls this an “evidence diversity” or agreement score, but calculates an average of directional values: low concentration, bullish evidence and bullish market signals map to 1; medium or neutral map to 0.5; high concentration or bearish map to 0. Failed or skipped signals are omitted. Consequently, unanimous bearish signals can score 0, while strongly opposing signals can average 0.5. It is neither a source-diversity measure nor a reliable agreement metric. The disagreement label can therefore be misleading. Rename or replace the calculation before treating it as a trust feature.

Material gaps before a broader pilot

  • Financial accuracy: replace LLM retirement arithmetic with deterministic calculations and explicit nominal versus real assumptions. Validate strategy coverage so every holding receives a valid result.
  • Evidence quality: automatically test whether cited passages support claims, retain precise provenance and assess extraction failures. Current citations are substantially prompt-driven.
  • Sessions and abuse controls: browser tokens are in localStorage; the API has no application request rate limiting. Harden session handling and resource limits.
  • Untrusted inputs: evaluate prompt injection from documents and chat, and keep retrieved text separate from executable instructions.
  • Freshness and analytics: expose quote timestamps and stale fallback indicators; calculate date-aligned return correlations and strengthen risk modeling.
  • Operations: add observability, cost attribution, provider quotas, backup and restore, migrations and deployment verification. Persist source and model configuration with each decision.

The decision journal is a useful application record, but is not an immutable compliance archive. Portfolio strategies store a fixed evidence score of 0.8 and use health score divided by 100 as the record’s confidence field, so journal scores are not comparable across result types.

The repository includes tests for authentication, user isolation, guardrails, SEC parsing, routing, documents and provider errors. Tests mock AI and market data. They support application behavior checks, but do not prove live model faithfulness or investment performance. This brief does not report a fresh test run or a live end-to-end validation.

11 Pilot decision and roadmap

Recommended CEO decision. Sponsor a limited decision support pilot after correcting the score semantics and validating the core evidence workflow. Agree a target user group, a representative question set and acceptance criteria before adding integrations or expanding distribution.

MeasureHow to assessPurpose

Retrieval coverageCheck known relevant filing and private-note passages on a benchmark.Reveal keyword retrieval misses.

Targets should be agreed with the pilot owner; no baseline, ROI or acceptance threshold has been measured here. Track cost per completed task, including retries and comparison fan-out, rather than only cost per model call.

Proposed delivery sequence

  1. Validate the foundation. Correct confidence and agreement semantics; verify citations; make retirement calculations deterministic; strengthen output schemas and data freshness.
  2. Improve research quality. Add metadata-rich retrieval, evaluate hybrid lexical and vector search, and benchmark chunking and reranking.
  3. Harden the pilot. Improve sessions, rate limits, privacy controls, telemetry and reproducible decision records.
  4. Expand from evidence. Consider account synchronization, advisor workflows and production hosting only after the pilot demonstrates useful, reliable behavior.

Likely CEO questions

Does it buy or sell securities? No. It produces analysis and action labels; users decide and execute elsewhere.

Why agents instead of one prompt? Specialized components let code calculate exact values while models interpret evidence. Intent routing avoids unnecessary work and parallelism can reduce elapsed time. The benefit should still be measured against a simpler baseline.

Can we trust the answer? The app exposes sources and opposing signals. Trust must be earned through claim-level evaluation, numerical validation and clear missing-data behavior; confidence alone is insufficient.

What is the moat? A validated research experience, useful portfolio context and high-quality permission-aware evidence could become an advantage. The current technology stack alone is not a moat.

What does it cost? Costs include model tokens, external data access, hosting and support. The app currently has no complete cost telemetry, so estimate economics from pilot task traces rather than inventing a price.
